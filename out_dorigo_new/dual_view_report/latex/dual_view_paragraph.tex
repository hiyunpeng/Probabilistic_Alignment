\paragraph{Dual-view attribution: attainment vs progress.}
We compare success-curve attribution (threshold attainment) with normalised-fitness attribution (sub-threshold progress) across 8 evaluated algorithms. The two views disagree on 5/8 methods, indicating that binary success can collapse information in strict regimes while value-based curves preserve progress dynamics. In particular, methods labelled \texttt{FLAT} under success-only but non-\texttt{FLAT} under fitness (progress without attainment) are: \texttt{ALO}, \texttt{BA}, \texttt{FA}, \texttt{MFO}. A notable cross-family disagreement is \texttt{PSO} in success but \texttt{ES} in fitness: \texttt{GWO}. This motivates reporting both views: success captures whether a method crosses a deployment-relevant threshold, while normalised fitness captures how efficiently a method converts additional budget into optimisation progress below that threshold.
